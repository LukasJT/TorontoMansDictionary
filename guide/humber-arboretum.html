The Humber Arboretum

Established in the 1970s as a partnership between Humber College and the Toronto region's conservation authority, the Humber Arboretum combines a public garden with an active horticultural teaching site.

Advertisement

The Humber Arboretum was established in the 1970s along the Humber River in northwest Toronto, developed through a partnership between Humber College and the region's conservation authority, combining an ecological restoration and flood-management mandate with an active teaching role for the college's horticulture and landscape programs. The site sits partly within the Humber River floodplain, giving it both an educational and a practical flood-mitigation function.

A working garden and classroom

Unlike a purely ornamental public garden, the Humber Arboretum has always operated with a dual purpose: providing public green space and demonstration gardens while functioning as a genuine outdoor classroom and research site for horticulture students. That combination has made it a somewhat unusual entry among Toronto's public gardens, more directly tied to an educational institution's ongoing curriculum than most of the city's other park spaces.
