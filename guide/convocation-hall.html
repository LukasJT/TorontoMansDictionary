Convocation Hall

Opened in 1907 after fire destroyed the University of Toronto's previous convocation space, Convocation Hall's distinctive dome has hosted graduations, first-year lectures, and touring concerts for over a century.

Advertisement

Convocation Hall opened in 1907 at the University of Toronto, built to replace convocation and lecture space lost when fire destroyed University College's original hall in 1890. Designed with a distinctive domed roof and semicircular seating plan, the building was engineered to hold large crowds for graduation ceremonies and public lectures, becoming one of the largest indoor gathering spaces on campus in an era before the university had anything resembling a modern arena or convention centre.

More than graduations

Beyond convocation ceremonies, the hall has served for over a century as a venue for oversized first-year lecture courses, public addresses by visiting dignitaries, and touring concerts and events too large for smaller campus venues. Its dome and rounded seating bank made it instantly recognizable to generations of University of Toronto students, many of whom sat through their largest introductory courses inside it before ever setting foot inside for their own graduation.
