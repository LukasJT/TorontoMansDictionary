A real former power station turned into a gallery for cutting-edge art

The Power Plant Contemporary Art Gallery

Housed in a genuine converted ice-making power station at Harbourfront Centre, the Power Plant has operated since the mid-1980s as one of Toronto's few galleries dedicated exclusively to contemporary art.

Advertisement

The Power Plant Contemporary Art Gallery occupies a genuine former power station at Harbourfront Centre, originally built to generate power for an ice-making plant that served the surrounding industrial waterfront before that area's redevelopment into public cultural space. The building's industrial brick smokestack, retained through the conversion, remains a recognizable landmark along the waterfront and a visual cue to the site's working past.

A gallery with no permanent collection

The gallery opened in its current form in the mid-1980s with a mandate focused exclusively on contemporary art, deliberately choosing not to build a permanent collection and instead running a continuous program of temporary exhibitions featuring Canadian and international contemporary artists. That curatorial focus has made the Power Plant one of the relatively few Toronto institutions dedicated specifically to contemporary art rather than art history more broadly, distinct in mandate from larger encyclopedic museums like the AGO.
