Lawrence Park

Developed starting in 1909 as a deliberately upscale garden suburb, Lawrence Park's original restrictive covenants shaped it into one of midtown Toronto's most affluent, architecturally consistent neighbourhoods.

Advertisement

Lawrence Park was developed starting in 1909 by developer Charles Boeckh as a deliberately upscale "garden suburb," part of an early-20th-century planning trend that favoured curving streets, generous setbacks and consistent architectural standards over the dense grid layouts of older city neighbourhoods. Boeckh imposed restrictive building covenants on the original lots, controlling house size, materials and setbacks to maintain a consistent, high-end character from the outset rather than leaving development to individual builders' discretion.

An intact early planned suburb

Those early controls, combined with the neighbourhood's proximity to the subway and good schools, helped Lawrence Park develop into one of midtown Toronto's most consistently affluent neighbourhoods, its tree-lined streets and large early-20th-century houses remaining largely intact more than a century after the original development began. It's frequently cited by planning historians as one of the best-preserved examples of the early-1900s garden suburb model in the city.
