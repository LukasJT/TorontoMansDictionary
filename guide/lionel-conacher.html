Voted Canada's male athlete of the half-century for excelling at almost every sport he tried

Lionel Conacher

Lionel Conacher played professional hockey, football and lacrosse, and semi-professional baseball and boxing, from a Toronto base — a versatility that earned him the title of Canada's male athlete of the first half of the 20th century.

Advertisement

Lionel Conacher grew up in Toronto and built a professional sports career unusual even by the looser standards of early-20th-century athletics: he played professional hockey, winning a Stanley Cup, and professional Canadian football, winning multiple Grey Cups, while also competing at a high level in lacrosse, baseball and boxing across the 1920s and 1930s. Few athletes before or since have sustained genuine excellence across so many different sports simultaneously.

Athlete of the half-century

That versatility earned Conacher the title of Canada's male athlete of the first half of the 20th century, voted by Canadian sportswriters in 1950, a recognition of a level of multi-sport dominance that later specialization in professional athletics made essentially impossible to replicate. Conacher later moved into politics, serving as a member of Parliament, before dying in 1954 while playing in a charity softball game, a fittingly athletic end for a man whose entire life had been organized around sport.
