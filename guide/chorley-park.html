Chorley Park

Ontario's Lieutenant Governor lived in a grand chateau-style residence on a Rosedale ravine bluff for just over thirty years before the government demolished it in 1961 rather than keep paying to maintain it.

Advertisement

Chorley Park was the site of Government House, a grand chateau-style residence built for Ontario's Lieutenant Governor overlooking the Rosedale ravine, completed in 1915. The building was designed on a genuinely lavish scale for a largely ceremonial vice-regal role, drawing criticism even at the time of its construction over the cost of building and maintaining such an elaborate residence for a position with limited actual governing power.

Demolished rather than maintained

The province stopped using the building as the Lieutenant Governor's residence in the 1930s amid Depression-era budget pressure, and after sitting largely vacant or used for temporary purposes over the following decades, the Ontario government demolished the house entirely in 1961 rather than continue paying for its upkeep, a decision heritage advocates have long cited as a significant architectural loss. The site is now maintained as public parkland under the Chorley Park name, offering sweeping views over the ravine with almost no trace remaining of the grand residence that once stood there.
