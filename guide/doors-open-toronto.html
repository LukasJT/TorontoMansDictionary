A weekend when buildings that are normally closed to the public let everyone in for free

Doors Open Toronto

Launched in 2000, Doors Open Toronto gives the public free access to buildings normally closed to visitors, from historic churches to modern office towers, for one weekend each spring.

Advertisement

Doors Open Toronto launched in 2000, part of a broader international movement of "doors open" events that started in France in the 1980s and spread to cities worldwide, offering the public free access for one weekend each year to buildings that are normally closed, private, or require paid admission. Participating sites have ranged from historic churches and government buildings to modern corporate headquarters and infrastructure sites not normally open for casual public tours.

A citywide architecture and history showcase

The event grew into one of the larger recurring cultural programs in Toronto's civic calendar, with dozens of participating buildings each year and volunteer guides often providing historical context that visitors wouldn't otherwise have access to. Doors Open Toronto has become a significant vehicle for public engagement with the city's architecture and history, letting residents see behind doors — literal and institutional — that stay closed the rest of the year.
