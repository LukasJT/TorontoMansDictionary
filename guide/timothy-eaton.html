An Irish immigrant whose fixed-price, no-haggling store rewrote Canadian retail

Timothy Eaton

Timothy Eaton opened his first Toronto dry goods store in 1869 on the then-radical promise of fixed prices, cash only, and money-back guarantees, building the T. Eaton Co. into a national institution.

Advertisement

Timothy Eaton emigrated from Ireland to Canada as a young man and opened a small dry goods store on Yonge Street in Toronto in 1869, built around a set of retail practices that were genuinely unusual for the time: fixed prices instead of haggling, cash payment instead of credit, and a money-back guarantee if customers weren't satisfied. Those policies, combined with an early and aggressive embrace of print advertising and mail-order catalogues, let Eaton's grow quickly from a single storefront into a retail chain with national reach.

A retail dynasty

The company Eaton founded, later known simply as Eaton's, became one of the dominant forces in Canadian retail for over a century, run by successive generations of the Eaton family until the company's eventual decline and 1999 bankruptcy. Timothy Eaton himself died in 1907, decades before the company reached its mid-20th-century peak, but the fixed-price, guarantee-backed retail model he introduced in that first Yonge Street store became the industry standard well beyond his own company's eventual collapse.
