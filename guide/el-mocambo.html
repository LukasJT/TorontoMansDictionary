The El Mocambo

Famous for its glowing neon palm tree sign, the El Mocambo hosted a legendary secret 1977 Rolling Stones show that became one of the most famous nights in Toronto music history.

Advertisement

The El Mocambo opened on Spadina Avenue in the 1940s, later becoming a live music club known for its distinctive rooftop neon sign featuring two glowing palm trees, one of the most recognizable pieces of commercial signage in downtown Toronto for decades. The club built a reputation through the 1970s and 80s as a key stop for touring rock acts and a launching pad for Canadian bands working their way up.

A secret Rolling Stones show

The club's most famous night came in March 1977, when the Rolling Stones played two intimate, officially unannounced shows there, recorded partly for the band's live album Love You Live, in a performance that became legendary specifically because almost no one in the audience knew in advance who was actually going to be playing. The El Mocambo went through periods of closure and financial trouble in later decades before being restored and reopened, its neon palm trees relit as a deliberately preserved piece of Toronto music history.
