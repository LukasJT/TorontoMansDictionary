The 1999 Snow Army

After a brutal series of snowstorms buried the city in January 1999, Mayor Mel Lastman formally requested Canadian Armed Forces help clearing snow — a decision that made Toronto a national punchline for years.

Advertisement

A series of major snowstorms hit Toronto in January 1999, dumping well over a metre of snow on the city in under two weeks and overwhelming the municipal snow-clearing equipment and budget that a normally milder lake-effect winter didn't require. With streets impassable and the TTC struggling to keep buses and streetcars running, Mayor Mel Lastman made the unusual decision to formally request assistance from the Canadian Armed Forces, which sent personnel and equipment, including armoured vehicles, to help clear snow from city streets.

A punchline that stuck

The request drew immediate national mockery, with commentators and rival-city residents across Canada — many of whom dealt with far heavier annual snowfall without military intervention — treating it as proof that Toronto couldn't handle ordinary winter weather. The jokes proved remarkably durable: "Toronto called in the army for snow" remains one of the most frequently repeated pieces of Toronto-mocking trivia decades later, even though the storm itself was genuinely severe by the city's normal standards and the request was, by most practical accounts, a reasonable emergency response rather than an overreaction.
