A decaying Bloor Street cinema restored after decades of near-collapse

The Paradise Theatre

Opened in 1937, the Paradise Theatre on Bloor Street fell into serious disrepair by the 2000s before a lengthy restoration reopened it as a modern cinema and event venue behind its original Art Moderne facade.

Advertisement

The Paradise Theatre opened in 1937 on Bloor Street West, its streamlined Art Moderne facade and marquee reflecting the architectural fashions of late-1930s cinema design. It operated as a working movie theatre for decades before falling into serious disrepair by the early 2000s, its building deteriorating to the point where it sat closed and increasingly derelict for an extended period, a visible symbol of decline on an otherwise busy commercial street.

A long-delayed restoration

A lengthy restoration project eventually returned the Paradise to working condition, preserving its distinctive original facade and marquee while rebuilding the interior as a modern cinema and event space capable of hosting film screenings, concerts and private events. The theatre's revival became a widely cited example of heritage building restoration in Toronto, turning what had been a near-total loss back into an active, architecturally distinctive piece of the Bloor Street streetscape.
