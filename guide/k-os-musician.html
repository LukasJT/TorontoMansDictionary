A rapper who built his sound on live instrumentation instead of samples

k-os

Toronto-raised k-os built a genre-blending sound combining hip hop, rock and reggae with live instrumentation, becoming one of Canadian hip hop's more musically distinct voices through the 2000s.

Advertisement

k-os, born Kevin Brereton, grew up moving between Whitby and Toronto before building a music career that deliberately blurred genre lines, combining hip hop rhythms and lyricism with rock guitar, reggae influences and live instrumentation rather than the sample-heavy production more typical of the genre at the time. That approach set him apart from many of his Canadian hip hop contemporaries from the start of his recording career in the late 1990s.

A distinct voice in Canadian music

Albums including Joyful Rebellion (2004) and Atlantis: Hymns for Disco (2006) built k-os a strong following and multiple Juno Award wins, with singles that found radio success across both hip hop and rock formats, reflecting his genre-crossing approach. His sound offered a distinct alternative within the broader Toronto hip hop scene, less oriented toward the traditional rap production of contemporaries and more toward a live-band, genre-fusion sensibility.
