The creek that gave a pioneer village, and a notorious intersection, its name

Black Creek

Black Creek, a tributary of the Humber River, lent its name to both Black Creek Pioneer Village and the Jane and Finch area, two very different pieces of Toronto identity tied to the same waterway.

Advertisement

Black Creek is a tributary of the Humber River, running through Toronto's northwest through neighbourhoods including Jane and Finch before joining the Humber near Black Creek Pioneer Village, the living-history museum that takes its name directly from the waterway running alongside it. The creek's valley, like much of Toronto's ravine network, remained undeveloped even as the surrounding land filled in with postwar apartment towers and subdivisions through the 1960s.

A name attached to very different places

The creek's name has become attached to two quite different pieces of Toronto identity: the pioneer village, a preserved 19th-century settlement showcasing early Ontario rural life, and the broader Jane and Finch area, a dense, ethnically diverse neighbourhood that developed its own distinct identity and reputation through the postwar decades. Both draw their name from the same modest waterway, a reminder of how much of Toronto's geography and neighbourhood naming still traces back to its original creek and river valleys.
