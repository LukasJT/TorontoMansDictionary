A door-to-door transit service born from a system that wasn't built for everyone

Wheel-Trans

Launched in 1975, Wheel-Trans gave Toronto residents with disabilities a dedicated door-to-door transit service at a time when the subway and streetcar network remained largely inaccessible.

Advertisement

Wheel-Trans launched in 1975 as a dedicated door-to-door transit service for Toronto residents with disabilities, created because the TTC's existing subway, streetcar and bus network remained largely inaccessible to riders using wheelchairs or with other mobility limitations, decades before accessibility retrofits became standard practice. The service used specially equipped vehicles to provide trip-by-trip, pre-booked transportation rather than requiring riders to navigate an inaccessible fixed-route system.

Growing alongside a slowly modernizing subway

Wheel-Trans grew substantially over the following decades as demand increased, even as the TTC gradually retrofitted subway stations and bought accessible buses and streetcars, work that took many more decades to reach full accessibility across the network. The service has faced persistent criticism over wait times and booking difficulties even as it has expanded, remaining an essential, if imperfect, part of Toronto's transit system for riders the rest of the network still doesn't fully accommodate.
