The Yonge Street Riot of 1992

A May 4, 1992 protest against the Rodney King verdict and a local police shooting escalated into looting along Yonge Street, and led to the province's landmark Stephen Lewis report on racism.

Advertisement

On May 4, 1992, a demonstration outside the United States consulate on University Avenue turned into a night of rioting along Yonge Street. The immediate spark was the acquittal, days earlier, of the Los Angeles police officers filmed beating Rodney King, which set off unrest across North America. In Toronto that anger fed directly into a local grievance: the recent police shooting of a young Black man, Raymond Lawrence, which followed a string of controversial police shootings of Black Torontonians through the late 1980s. The Black Action Defense Committee organized the demonstration to protest both the King verdict and the pattern of local shootings; as the crowd moved onto Yonge Street, the protest escalated into looting and vandalism near the Eaton Centre.

The Stephen Lewis report

In the aftermath, then-premier Bob Rae asked Stephen Lewis, a former Ontario NDP leader and Canadian ambassador to the United Nations, to report on race relations in the province. Lewis delivered his report within weeks and was blunt: what happened on Yonge Street, he wrote, was not really about Los Angeles, but about the accumulated frustration of Toronto's Black community with policing and systemic racism closer to home. The report is widely credited as one of the first prominent government-commissioned Canadian documents to name anti-Black racism directly, and it fed into the creation of Ontario's Anti-Racism Secretariat and a wave of subsequent reviews of policing practices in the province.
