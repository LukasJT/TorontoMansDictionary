A secret WWII spy school on Lake Ontario, with a real connection to James Bond

Camp X

Built in 1941 near Whitby, Camp X trained Allied agents in espionage and sabotage during the Second World War, and one of its organizers, Ian Fleming, later drew on the experience to create James Bond.

Advertisement

Camp X opened in 1941 on the Lake Ontario shoreline near Whitby, east of Toronto, established as a secret training facility for Allied intelligence agents during the Second World War. Operated with British intelligence involvement, the camp taught espionage tradecraft, sabotage, unarmed combat and covert communications to agents preparing for operations in occupied Europe, chosen for its relatively isolated lakeside location within reach of Toronto but away from public attention.

A real link to a fictional spy

British intelligence officer Ian Fleming, who later created James Bond, was involved with training programs connected to Camp X's network during the war, and Fleming himself is reported to have visited the site, an experience widely cited as part of the real-world background that later informed his fiction. The camp was decommissioned after the war and largely demolished, but its role training real wartime agents, with a genuine connection to one of the 20th century's most famous fictional spies, has made it a well-documented, if lesser-known, piece of the Toronto region's wartime history.
