A Victorian glass conservatory that survived its own park's decline

Allan Gardens

Donated to the city in 1858 by mayor George William Allan, Allan Gardens grew into a Victorian glass-domed conservatory that has outlasted repeated fires and the surrounding neighbourhood's decades of decline.

Advertisement

Allan Gardens takes its name from George William Allan, a former mayor of Toronto who donated part of his family estate to the city in 1858 for use as a public park and botanical garden. The park's landmark glass conservatory, known as the Palm House, opened in 1910, replacing an earlier greenhouse structure destroyed by fire in 1902, and its Victorian domed design made it one of the more architecturally distinctive public buildings in the city's park system.

A park through decline and renewal

The surrounding downtown-east neighbourhood went through significant decline through the mid-to-late 20th century, and Allan Gardens itself became associated for a period with homelessness and drug activity in the surrounding blocks, even as the conservatory continued operating as a free public greenhouse year-round. The conservatory remains an active attraction today, its collection of tropical and desert plants offering one of the only free indoor botanical spaces in downtown Toronto.
