Toronto's wealthiest street, named for the horse trails that ran through it

The Bridle Path

Developed starting in the 1920s on former farmland, the Bridle Path became synonymous with extreme wealth in Toronto, its name preserving the equestrian trails that once wound through the estate lots.

Advertisement

The Bridle Path takes its name literally: the winding streets running through this stretch of North York, north of York Mills Road, follow the paths of horse riding trails that once wound through the area's large rural estate lots before the land was subdivided for luxury housing starting in the 1920s and continuing through the mid-20th century. The naturally winding, curved street layout, unusual for a Toronto suburb, still traces those original bridle paths.

Synonymous with wealth

The neighbourhood developed into a cluster of some of the largest and most expensive private residences in Canada, built on multi-acre lots that allowed for a scale of house and grounds rarely available closer to downtown. "The Bridle Path" became local shorthand for extreme wealth in Toronto more broadly, a status the neighbourhood has held for decades, home over the years to prominent business figures, entertainers and, at various points, professional athletes drawn by its privacy and scale.
