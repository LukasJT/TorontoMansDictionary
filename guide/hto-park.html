HTO Park

Opened in 2007 on former parking lot land, HTO Park brought striped umbrellas and imported sand to Toronto's central waterfront, its name a nod to the H2O it deliberately doesn't let you swim in.

Advertisement

HTO Park opened in 2007 on Toronto's central waterfront, built on land that had previously served as a surface parking lot, part of the earliest wave of public space projects delivered under Waterfront Toronto's redevelopment mandate. Designed by Claude Cormier + Associés, the same firm behind Sugar Beach, the park brought imported sand and striped umbrellas to create a beach-like atmosphere directly on the water's edge, despite the lake itself not being safe or intended for swimming at that location.

A playful name

The park's name is a play on H2O, the chemical formula for water, reflecting the design team's sense of humour about building a beach experience where actual swimming wasn't the point. HTO Park became one of the early proof points that Toronto's long-underused central waterfront could be turned into genuinely popular public space, helping build momentum and public support for the larger, more expensive waterfront redevelopment projects that followed it.
