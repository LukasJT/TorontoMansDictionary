The Horseshoe Tavern

Opened in 1947 as a country and western bar, the Horseshoe Tavern shifted with the times through rockabilly, punk and indie rock, staying one of Toronto's most consistently important live music venues for over seven decades.

Advertisement

The Horseshoe Tavern opened on Queen Street West in 1947 as a country and western bar, drawing touring American country acts through the 1950s and 60s at a time when Toronto had few dedicated live music venues of any kind. The club shifted with changing musical tastes over the following decades, becoming a key stop for rockabilly acts in the 1970s before playing a central role in Toronto's punk scene later that same decade, hosting bands including the Ramones during their first Toronto shows.

Still central after seven decades

The Horseshoe continued adapting through the 1980s, 90s and 2000s, becoming a proving ground for Toronto indie and alternative bands and a regular stop for touring acts on the way up, hosting early shows by artists who later became internationally known. Its survival across so many shifts in musical fashion, on a stretch of Queen Street that has itself changed dramatically around it, has made the Horseshoe one of the most consistently cited "if these walls could talk" venues in Canadian music history.
