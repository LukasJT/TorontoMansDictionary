Toronto Paramedic Services

The 1998 municipal amalgamation forced six separate ambulance services, each run by a different pre-amalgamation municipality, to merge into one citywide paramedic service almost overnight.

Advertisement

Before Toronto's 1998 amalgamation, ambulance service was run separately by each of the six municipalities that made up Metro Toronto, including Scarborough, North York and Etobicoke, each with its own dispatch system, equipment standards and staffing practices. Amalgamation forced those six separate services to merge into a single citywide operation essentially overnight, one of the more logistically complicated pieces of the broader municipal merger.

One service, one standard

The consolidated service, which became Toronto Paramedic Services, standardized dispatch, training and equipment across the newly unified city, addressing inconsistencies that had existed between the pre-amalgamation municipalities' separate systems. It now operates as one of the largest municipal paramedic services in Canada, responding to hundreds of thousands of calls annually across the full expanse of the amalgamated city, a scale and level of coordination that would have been organizationally impossible under the old six-service structure.
