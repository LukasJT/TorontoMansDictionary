Deer Park

Deer Park takes its name from an actual deer herd kept on a 19th-century estate near St. Clair and Yonge, long since subdivided into one of midtown Toronto's older residential neighbourhoods.

Advertisement

Deer Park takes its unusually literal name from an estate established in the mid-19th century near present-day St. Clair Avenue and Yonge Street, whose owner kept an actual herd of deer on the grounds, a detail that stuck as a place name long after the estate itself was subdivided. The area developed gradually through the late 19th and early 20th centuries as Toronto's urban grid pushed north along Yonge Street, absorbing what had been a semi-rural estate district into the city's built-up residential fabric.

A quiet midtown neighbourhood

Deer Park today sits just south of Summerhill and north of Rosedale, a residential neighbourhood of mixed housing types built up incrementally rather than following a single developer's master plan the way Lawrence Park or Wychwood Park did. Its name remains one of several Toronto place names, alongside Deer Park itself and nearby Rathnelly, that trace back to the private estates and land grants that predated the city's modern street grid.
