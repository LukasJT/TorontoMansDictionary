Centreville Amusement Park

Opened in 1967 on the Toronto Islands, Centreville was designed as a deliberately old-fashioned, low-key amusement park scaled to young children, reachable only by ferry.

Advertisement

Centreville Amusement Park opened in 1967 on Centre Island, part of the Toronto Islands, built as a deliberately old-fashioned, small-scale amusement park designed around a mock 19th-century village theme rather than the large thrill rides that defined most contemporary amusement parks. Its scale and gentle rides were aimed specifically at young children and families, avoiding the roller coasters and high-speed attractions that larger mainland parks were built around.

A ferry-only, car-free park

Centreville's island location, reachable only by ferry, kept it deliberately separate from car-dependent mainland amusement parks, part of a broader vision for the Toronto Islands as a car-free recreational escape close to downtown. The park has continued operating in roughly the same low-key, family-oriented form for decades, its antique carousel and small-scale rides giving generations of Toronto children an early, gentle introduction to amusement park rides before graduating to larger attractions elsewhere.
