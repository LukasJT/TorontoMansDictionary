The Beaches International Jazz Festival

Launched in the 1990s, the Beaches International Jazz Festival turns Queen Street East and the boardwalk into a series of free outdoor jazz stages each July, a lower-key counterpart to Toronto's main jazz festival downtown.

Advertisement

The Beaches International Jazz Festival launched in the 1990s in the Beaches neighbourhood, organized as a free, outdoor, community-scale alternative to the larger ticketed jazz festival held downtown. Musicians perform on a series of stages set up along Queen Street East and the neighbourhood's lakeside boardwalk, drawing crowds who wander between performances rather than committing to a single ticketed venue for the evening.

A neighbourhood festival that grew regionally

The festival's free, walkable format made it one of the more accessible major music events in the city, and it grew over the following decades into one of the larger annual draws to the Beaches, with the neighbourhood's restaurants and shops benefiting from the crowds the festival brought in each July. Its lower-key, neighbourhood-rooted character distinguishes it from Toronto's broader jazz history along the old Yonge Street club circuit, offering a more casual, street-level entry point into live jazz for audiences who might not otherwise seek out a club show.
