A superstore chain built by absorbing its own biggest competitor

Indigo Books & Music

Founded in Toronto in 1996 by Heather Reisman, Indigo grew into Canada's dominant bookstore chain by merging with its rival Chapters in 2001.

Advertisement

Indigo Books & Music was founded in Toronto in 1996 by Heather Reisman, a businesswoman who set out to build a large-format bookstore chain modelled partly on American superstore chains, combining books with music, gifts and a café-style browsing experience. Indigo opened its early flagship stores in the Toronto area before expanding across the country through the late 1990s, competing directly against Chapters, a rival superstore chain that had launched a few years earlier and grown quickly of its own.

Absorbing the competition

Rather than compete indefinitely, Indigo acquired Chapters in 2001 in a deal that consolidated Canada's big-box bookstore market under a single company, operating both the Chapters and Indigo banners alongside the smaller Coles chain. The combined company, headquartered in Toronto, became Canada's dominant book retailer, later expanding into general merchandise and lifestyle products as book sales shifted online, while remaining one of the few large physical bookstore chains to survive the retail disruption that hit the sector across North America.
