Toronto's Raccoons

Toronto's dense urban raccoon population, drawn by abundant food waste and few natural predators, forced the city to design and redesign its green compost bins specifically to outsmart them.

Advertisement

Toronto has, for decades, carried an informal reputation as one of the world's most raccoon-dense cities, a status researchers have linked to the animal's intelligence, dexterity and comfort living alongside humans, combined with the abundant food waste, mature tree cover and near-total absence of natural predators that a large North American city like Toronto provides. Studies of urban raccoon behaviour have specifically used Toronto's population as a research subject given how thoroughly the animals have adapted to city life here.

An arms race over the green bin

That adaptability became a genuine municipal design problem when Toronto rolled out its curbside green bin composting program in the 2000s: early bin designs were quickly defeated by raccoons capable of learning and manipulating simple latches to access food waste inside. The city redesigned the bins multiple times, adding increasingly raccoon-resistant locking mechanisms that required a level of dexterity or leverage the animals couldn't easily replicate, a low-grade design battle between municipal engineers and the city's raccoon population that received international media coverage and cemented Toronto's reputation as a capital of urban raccoon culture.
