The Noronic Fire

A fire tore through the passenger ship SS Noronic while it was docked in Toronto Harbour overnight in September 1949, killing at least 118 people in the deadliest single disaster in the city's history.

Advertisement

The SS Noronic, a large Great Lakes passenger cruise ship, was docked overnight at a Toronto Harbour pier on September 17, 1949, carrying more than 500 passengers on a cruise from Detroit, when a fire broke out in a linen closet and spread with extraordinary speed through the ship's wooden interior finishes. Most passengers were asleep at the time, and the fire's rapid spread through corridors and stairwells trapped many before they could reach the deck or lifeboats moored alongside the pier.

Toronto's deadliest disaster

At least 118 people died in the fire, the overwhelming majority of them passengers rather than crew, making it the deadliest single disaster in Toronto's history, a distinction it still holds. The disaster prompted sweeping changes to marine fire safety regulations across the Great Lakes and internationally, including new requirements for fire-resistant construction materials, improved fire detection systems, and stricter lifeboat and emergency drill standards on passenger vessels, most of which the Noronic itself had lacked.
