The British Methodist Episcopal Church

Formed in the 1850s after Black congregants split from a Methodist denomination that wouldn't treat them as full equals, the British Methodist Episcopal Church became a central institution for Black Torontonians.

Advertisement

The British Methodist Episcopal Church formed in the 1850s after Black members of existing Methodist congregations, many of them formerly enslaved people who had escaped to Upper Canada via the Underground Railroad, found themselves still relegated to separate seating and denied full participation and leadership roles within predominantly white congregations. Rather than continue seeking equal treatment within existing denominations, Black congregants organized their own independent church body.

A central institution for Black Toronto

The denomination's Toronto congregations became central gathering places and organizing hubs for the city's Black community through the 19th and 20th centuries, hosting not just religious services but community meetings, social events and, at various points, political organizing tied to civil rights causes. Several of the church's original Toronto buildings remain standing and in use today, among the oldest continuously Black-led religious institutions in Canada.
