Canada's first global sports superstar, a Toronto Islands rower who was world champion

Ned Hanlan

Ned Hanlan, raised on Toronto Island, became the world champion single sculls rower of the late 19th century, drawing massive crowds and making him arguably Canada's first genuine international sports celebrity.

Advertisement

Ned Hanlan grew up on Toronto Island in the mid-19th century, the son of a hotel keeper, learning to row on the same waters that would later make him famous. He turned to competitive single sculls rowing as a young man and rose rapidly through Canadian, then international competition, developing an unconventional sliding-seat rowing technique that gave him a significant edge over rivals still using older rowing styles.

A world champion and Toronto's first sports icon

Hanlan won the world championship in single sculls in 1880 and successfully defended his title multiple times through the 1880s, racing in front of enormous crowds in Canada, the United States, Britain and Australia at a time when competitive rowing drew the kind of mass spectator attention professional sports would later command more broadly. His fame made him arguably Canada's first genuine international sports celebrity, and Hanlan's Point, the western tip of the Toronto Islands where he grew up, carries his name today, along with a statue commemorating his championship career.
