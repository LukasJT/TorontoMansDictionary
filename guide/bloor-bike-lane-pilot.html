The Bloor Street Bike Lane Pilot

A 2016 pilot project installed protected bike lanes on a stretch of Bloor Street after decades of advocacy — and the debate over making them permanent, and extending them, has continued ever since.

Advertisement

Cycling advocates had pushed for protected bike lanes on Bloor Street, one of Toronto's busiest east-west arterials, for decades before City Council approved a pilot project in 2016 to install them between Shaw Street and Avenue Road. The pilot followed years of failed attempts and studies dating back to the 1970s, and was framed explicitly as a data-gathering trial rather than a permanent commitment, a compromise that let council approve installation without fully resolving the underlying political disagreement over removing street parking and vehicle lanes for cyclists.

From trial to permanent, slowly

Studies of the pilot found increased cycling volumes and improved safety along the corridor without the severe business impacts opponents had predicted, evidence that helped build the case for making the lanes permanent in 2017 and for later extending protected bike lanes to other major Toronto streets, including sections of Yonge Street and University Avenue. The Bloor pilot became a widely cited reference case in Toronto's broader, still-ongoing debate over expanding protected cycling infrastructure across a road network built almost entirely around car traffic.
