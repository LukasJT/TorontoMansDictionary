A Riverdale green space built on land bought to keep it out of developers' hands

Withrow Park

The City of Toronto bought the land for Withrow Park in 1910, giving the growing Riverdale neighbourhood a large public green space that still anchors the area's community life today.

Advertisement

Withrow Park sits in the heart of Riverdale, near Leslieville, a large block of green space the City of Toronto purchased in 1910 as the surrounding streets were being built up with houses, at a time when city planners were increasingly conscious of setting aside parkland before residential development consumed all the available space. The park is named for John Jacob Withrow, a 19th-century Toronto builder and civic figure who had served as president of the Industrial Exhibition Association, the organization behind what became the CNE.

A neighbourhood anchor

Over the following century, Withrow Park grew into one of the east end's central gathering spaces, hosting a farmers' market, sports fields, an off-leash dog area, and a wading pool that make it a daily fixture for the surrounding Riverdale community. Its scale and central location within a dense, largely residential neighbourhood have made it one of Toronto's most consistently used park spaces outside the downtown core.
