A loose, revolving-door band that put Toronto indie rock on the map

Broken Social Scene

Formed in Toronto in 1999 with a rotating cast of up to twenty members, Broken Social Scene became the centre of a wider community of Toronto indie bands in the early 2000s.

Advertisement

Broken Social Scene formed in Toronto in 1999 around musicians Kevin Drew and Brendan Canning, built from the start as a loose collective rather than a fixed band, with a rotating roster that at various points included members of Metric, Feist, Stars and other Toronto acts. That structure made the band as much a hub for the city's indie music scene as a group in its own right, with members regularly stepping out to release solo or side-project work under their own names.

A defining Toronto sound

The band's 2002 album You Forgot It in People became a landmark record for Canadian indie rock, drawing international critical attention to Toronto's music scene at a moment when the city was not typically associated with cutting-edge indie music. The album's success helped establish Toronto, alongside Montreal, as a serious node in North American indie rock through the 2000s, and Broken Social Scene's collective model influenced how a generation of Toronto musicians thought about collaboration across bands.
