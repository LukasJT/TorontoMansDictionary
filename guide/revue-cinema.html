A community-owned repertory cinema saved by a petition and a co-op

The Revue Cinema

Opened in 1912 in Roncesvalles, the Revue Cinema faced closure in 2006 before a community campaign turned it into a non-profit, volunteer-supported repertory theatre run for the neighbourhood by the neighbourhood.

Advertisement

The Revue Cinema opened in 1912 in Roncesvalles, one of Toronto's early neighbourhood movie houses, operating for most of the 20th century as a commercial single-screen theatre showing a mix of first-run and repertory films. Like many small independent cinemas, it faced increasing financial pressure by the 2000s as multiplexes and home video eroded the audience for a small neighbourhood theatre.

Saved by its own neighbourhood

When the Revue faced closure in 2006, Roncesvalles residents organized a community campaign to save it, eventually forming a non-profit organization that took over the cinema's operation with significant volunteer support, an unusual ownership model for a working commercial cinema. The Revue has continued operating under that community-supported structure, programming repertory screenings and festival-style events that a purely for-profit theatre in the same location might not have prioritized, making it one of the more distinctive examples of grassroots cultural preservation in the city.
