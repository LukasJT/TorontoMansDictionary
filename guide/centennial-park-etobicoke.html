Centennial Park

Built in the late 1960s on former gravel pit land to mark Canada's centennial, Centennial Park grew into one of Etobicoke's largest sports and recreation complexes.

Advertisement

Centennial Park was developed in Etobicoke in the late 1960s on land that had previously been used for gravel extraction, part of a wave of municipal park projects across Canada timed to the country's 1967 centennial celebrations. Converting an exhausted gravel pit into usable parkland required substantial regrading and landscaping work, transforming an industrial site into recreational space rather than leaving it as unusable, disturbed land.

A major recreation hub

The park grew over subsequent decades into one of the largest recreational complexes in Etobicoke, eventually including sports fields, a ski hill built using material excavated from the original gravel pit, a golf course, and an equestrian centre, giving the western suburb a concentration of recreational facilities unusual for a single park site. Its origin as reclaimed industrial land, converted specifically as a centennial legacy project, ties it into the broader wave of Canadian civic infrastructure built to mark the 1967 anniversary.
