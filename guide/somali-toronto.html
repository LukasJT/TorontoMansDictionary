Somali Toronto

Somalia's collapse into civil war in 1991 sent large numbers of refugees to Toronto, particularly to apartment towers in Etobicoke, building one of the largest Somali communities anywhere outside East Africa.

Advertisement

Somalia's central government collapsed into civil war in 1991, triggering a mass displacement that sent hundreds of thousands of Somalis fleeing the country, with a significant number eventually settling in Toronto through the 1990s as refugees and refugee claimants. The scale and suddenness of that displacement gave Toronto's Somali community a different character than immigrant communities that had built up more gradually over generations, arriving instead in a compressed period of crisis-driven migration.

A community concentrated in Etobicoke's towers

Many Somali newcomers settled in apartment towers in Etobicoke, particularly around Dixon Road, building a dense community concentration that became one of the largest Somali populations in North America. The community has built mosques, community organizations and businesses reflecting its scale, while also navigating the particular challenges of a population that arrived overwhelmingly as refugees fleeing an active conflict rather than through more conventional immigration streams.
