An urban beach built next to a working sugar refinery

Sugar Beach

Opened in 2010 on the East Bayfront, Sugar Beach brought pink umbrellas and imported sand to a stretch of waterfront still overlooked by the Redpath Sugar refinery it's named for.

Advertisement

Sugar Beach opened in 2010 on Toronto's East Bayfront, one of the first major public spaces completed as part of Waterfront Toronto's long-running redevelopment of the city's central waterfront. Designed by landscape architecture firm Claude Cormier + Associés, the small artificial beach brought in imported sand and installed dozens of oversized pink umbrellas along the water's edge, a deliberately playful design that stood out against the industrial waterfront it sat within.

Named for its neighbour

The park takes its name from its immediate neighbour, the still-operating Redpath Sugar refinery next door, whose working industrial silos remain visible from the beach — an unusual and intentional juxtaposition of a leisure space next to active heavy industry that designers treated as a feature rather than something to screen out. Sugar Beach became one of the more photographed and widely cited successes of Toronto's waterfront revitalization, credited with demonstrating that small, design-forward public spaces could draw visitors even without swimmable water.
