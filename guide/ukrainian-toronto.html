A community built in waves, from early homesteaders to Cold War refugees

Ukrainian Toronto

Ukrainian immigration to Toronto came in distinct waves across more than a century, building institutions concentrated around Bloor West Village that remain central to one of Canada's largest Ukrainian communities.

Advertisement

Ukrainian immigration to Toronto arrived in several distinct waves, beginning with early-20th-century homesteaders and labourers, followed by a larger wave of refugees and displaced persons after the Second World War fleeing Soviet control of Ukraine, and later further waves following Ukrainian independence in 1991 and, more recently, Russia's invasion in 2022. Each wave brought a different mix of political and economic motivations, but collectively built Toronto into home to one of the largest Ukrainian communities outside Ukraine itself.

Institutions centred on Bloor West Village

Toronto's Ukrainian community built a dense network of churches, credit unions, cultural halls and businesses, with Bloor West Village in particular developing a strong concentration of Ukrainian shops, bakeries and restaurants that remains visible along its main commercial strip today. Annual events including a Ukrainian festival along Bloor Street have become fixtures of the city's summer festival calendar, and the community's institutions took on renewed visibility and urgency after 2022 as Toronto became a major resettlement destination for Ukrainians displaced by the war.
