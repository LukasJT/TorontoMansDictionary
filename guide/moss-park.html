A downtown neighbourhood named for a mansion that no longer exists

Moss Park

Named for a long-demolished 19th-century estate, Moss Park became a dense downtown-east neighbourhood defined by social housing, shelters, and, more recently, a supervised drug consumption site.

Advertisement

Moss Park takes its name from an early-19th-century estate house built by the Allan family, one of York's prominent early landowning families; the mansion itself was demolished long ago, but its name stuck to the surrounding downtown-east neighbourhood and to the park that occupies part of the original grounds today. The area redeveloped through the 20th century into a dense mix of social housing, including the Moss Park apartment towers built in the postwar decades, alongside shelters and social services concentrated in the surrounding blocks.

A frontline in the overdose crisis

Moss Park became a focal point of Toronto's response to the opioid overdose crisis in the 2010s, when community volunteers set up an unsanctioned overdose prevention tent in the park in 2017 in response to a rising death toll, pressuring the city and province to authorize permanent supervised consumption services faster than they otherwise might have moved. That grassroots intervention is widely credited with accelerating the opening of sanctioned supervised consumption sites in Toronto and elsewhere in Ontario.
